Day by day

Each day uses the clock where you are that day: home clock until departure, destination clock after landing. Times are rounded to 5 minutes. The melatonin item is for people who have already been advised to use it. The steady days at the start are grouped into one card.

Read this first

  • This is general information, not medical advice. If you have a medical condition, take regular medication, are pregnant, or work in a safety-critical role, check the plan with your doctor first.
  • Melatonin times are shown only for people who have already been advised to use it. In Australia melatonin is generally prescription-only, with some pharmacist-only exceptions, so ask your doctor or pharmacist what applies to you. Published studies have used doses from about 0.5 mg to 3 mg. Rules differ in the United States.
  • Shifting your sleep earlier can leave you tired in the days before the flight. Avoid driving or safety-critical tasks when sleep deprived, and treat every time here as a target, not a guarantee. Real body clocks usually shift slower than the plan assumes.
  • Bright light matters more than anything else in this plan. If the sun is not up during a bright light window, use a light box (these are often around 10,000 lux) or as much indoor light as you can. Outdoor daylight, even when overcast, is far brighter than indoor lighting.
  • Meal timing is the weakest part of the evidence. One human study found that shifting meals moved peripheral clocks but not the master clock markers, so treat meal times as a gentle aid only.
  • Defaults for this trip are written into this file. Keep the file off shared drives and cloud folders if the dates matter to you.

Method and sources

  • Rates of shifting: people can delay up to about 1.5 h per day and advance up to about 1 h per day. Eastward flights across 8 or more time zones can make the clock delay instead of advance. Burgess HJ. Using bright light and melatonin to reduce jet lag (patient guide).
  • Real travellers delayed about 92 min per day after westward flights and advanced about 57 min per day after eastward flights. Burgess HJ, Eastman CI. Prevention of jet lag. Sleep Medicine Clinics, 2009.
  • Light phase response curve: light in the roughly 12 h before CBTmin delays the clock, light in the roughly 12 h after advances it, with the largest shifts 3 to 6 h either side of CBTmin. Pre-flight advance: move bed and wake times earlier by 30 to 60 min per day, minimise evening light, maximise morning light, and time melatonin 6.5 h before bed (11.5 h before CBTmin). Roach GD, Sargent C. Interventions to minimize jet lag after westward and eastward flight. Frontiers in Physiology, 2019, 10:927.
  • Pre-adaptation protocols and melatonin timing: Eastman CI, Burgess HJ. How to travel the world without jet lag. Sleep Medicine Clinics, 2009. Arendt J. Managing jet lag: some of the problems and possible new solutions. Sleep Medicine Reviews, 2009.
  • When the time difference is near 12 h it is usually easier to delay than to advance. The plan therefore compares both directions and picks advance only when it needs 8 h or less.
  • Caffeine: 400 mg taken even 6 h before bed reduced sleep by about an hour. This plan uses 8 h by default. Drake C, Roehrs T, Shambroom J, Roth T. Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, 2013, 9(11):1195-1200.
  • Meal timing: Wehrens SMT et al. Meal timing regulates the human circadian system. Current Biology, 2017.
  • Assumptions you can change: body temperature low about 3 h before waking, 8 h sleep unless your wake and bed times say otherwise, and no body clock shift while in the air. Naps are limited to 30 min, in the early to mid afternoon.
